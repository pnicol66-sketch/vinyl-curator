Vinyl Curator — Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 October 2026

Vinyl Curator is a photography app for cataloguing vinyl records. It runs in your web browser on your own phone. There is no account to create, and no company collecting anything about you. The one thing the app's maker receives is the crop training data described below, which you can turn off.

The short version. Your album photographs and details stay on your phone and in your own Google Drive. The one thing sent to the app's maker is crop training data — a reduced copy of each photo you crop and the outline you set, with nothing that names you — and you can turn it off in Settings.

What the app stores, and where

  • On your phone. Your album photographs, the details you type (artist, title, grades, matrix numbers) and your settings are held in your browser's local storage on the device. They are not transmitted anywhere by the app except as this page describes. Crop training copies wait here until they are sent, and are then deleted from the phone.
  • In your Google Drive, if you choose. When you tap "Upload to Google Drive", the app copies that album's photographs into a folder in your own Google Drive account. You own those files.

What the app can see in your Google Drive

The app requests a single, narrow Google permission: drive.file. This limits it to files and folders it creates itself, or that you explicitly hand to it. It cannot see, read, or list anything else in your Drive.

Sharing a folder

Some copies of this app are set up to work with an archive run by a record curator. In that case, the first time you upload into a folder, the app asks whether to share that one folder with a named email address, which is shown to you in the prompt.

  • Sharing is read-only — the other person can view and download, not alter or delete.
  • It happens only if you agree. Decline and nothing is shared.
  • It covers that folder alone. Nothing else in your Drive is affected.
  • You remain the owner and can stop sharing at any time from Google Drive itself.

Crop training data (on unless you turn it off)

To make the app's automatic cropping better, each time you save a cropped photo the app keeps a copy and sends it to the app's maker the next time it is online. The first time the app opens with this in place, it tells you and asks whether to keep it on.

  • What is sent: the photograph as you took it, before cropping, reduced to 1280 pixels on its longest side and re-saved, so it carries no location or camera details; the outline you set on it and any rotation; which kind of shot it was (cover, label or run-out); whether you adjusted an automatic crop; the app version; and a reference number that groups one album's photos together.
  • What is not sent: no artist, title, notes, grades or prices, nothing from your Google Drive, and nothing that names you. The address your phone connects from is not stored with them; the service that receives them keeps a routine record of requests, including that address, for a few days, for troubleshooting.
  • Where it goes: to crops.vinylcurator.net, over an encrypted connection, into private storage only the app's maker can read. It is used only to improve the app's automatic cropping, and is never published, sold or shared.
  • To turn it off: Settings ⚙ → Crop training data → untick Send crop training data → Save settings. Nothing more is kept or sent, and copies not yet sent are deleted from your phone.
  • Because nothing that identifies you is sent with them, copies already sent cannot be picked out and deleted afterwards.

Checking a record before you buy (only if you set it up)

Settings ⚙ → Check a record lets you ask your appraiser about a record you are thinking of buying. It does nothing until you enter the check address your appraiser gives you. When you run a check:

  • The photos you take of the listing, and the details and asking price you enter, are sent to that check address (a Google Apps Script address run by your appraiser) and kept in your appraiser's records.
  • To prove who you are, the app asks Google to sign you in and sends that sign-in to the check address. It carries your email address only and gives no access to your Drive.
  • Your appraiser's sheet reads the photos with Anthropic's photo-reading service, on the reading key you entered. The key is stored only on this phone and is sent with each check, for that check only. Anthropic's handling is covered by the Anthropic Privacy Policy.
  • The answer comes back to your phone and is kept there with the check.

What the app sends over the internet

Only to these addresses:

  • accounts.google.com — to sign you in to your own Google account
  • www.googleapis.com — to upload your photographs to your own Drive
  • apis.google.com — only if you use the optional folder picker
  • crops.vinylcurator.net — crop training data, unless you turn it off
  • script.google.com — only if you use Check a record: the check address your appraiser gave you

Your Google sign-in for Drive is held in the page's memory while you use the app, is never written to storage, and is sent only to Google. A check's sign-in is held the same way and is sent only to Google and to the check address. Google's handling of your account is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, tracking, telemetry, or advertising of any kind
  • No cookies used for tracking
  • No selling of your data, and no sharing beyond what this page describes
  • No use of anything read from or saved to your Google Drive to develop, improve or train any artificial-intelligence or machine-learning model — crop training data is sent from the phone and never passes through your Drive
  • No collection of your location, contacts, or anything else on the device

The camera is used only while you are actively taking a photograph, and the image goes straight into the app's local storage on your phone.

Deleting your data

  • On the phone: Settings ⚙ → Delete ALL app data removes every album, photograph, record check, crop training copy and setting held locally.
  • In Google Drive: the uploaded folders are yours — delete them, or stop sharing them, from Drive directly.
  • Revoking access: you can remove the app's permission entirely at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Children

The app is not directed at children.
